How it decides

Do pushups and the page counts every rep and tells you why a rep was bad (hips sagging, hips too high, knees down, dropped to the floor, too shallow). Everything runs on your device: the pose model is downloaded once (about 20 MB) and no frame is ever uploaded. Ported from the Python original.

What it watches

MediaPipe PoseLandmarker (full) finds 33 body points on every frame, on your GPU when available.

What counts as a rep

Shoulder height tracks the movement. A rep is top → bottom → top; the dip must be at least a quarter of your torso length (shallower dips say "go lower" and do not count). Standing up, walking into the frame or dropping from a plank onto your knees to rest never count; a knee pushup (knees on the floor from the start) counts as an attempt and is graded "knees down".

How form is judged

Form is judged by geometry, in units of your own torso length so the camera distance and the way you face do not matter. Just before the descent and at the bottom: hips more than a fifth of a torso below the shoulder–ankle line is "hips sagging", above it is "hips too high", a knee angle under 130° is "knees down". At the bottom, averaged over the frames spent there: elbows still level with the shoulders (the body came down without the arms bending back) is "dropped to the floor", and a chest kept half a torso above the hands while the hips hang a fifth below the shoulders is "hips sagging" too.

When counting pauses

While a placement hint is on screen (head or feet cut off, two people, frontal view, too dark) counting is paused and the skeleton turns grey, so a half-visible body cannot produce a silent wrong count.

Why there is no neural network

There is no neural network any more. The classifier the Python original shipped (trained on one person's videos) was retired on 2026-09-19: held out honestly it scored that person's own unseen bad reps 50/50, called every clean rep of a second person bad, swung from 0.97 to 0.01 between neighbouring frames, and had been switched off for anyone facing the other way. Rules you can read beat a coin you cannot.

Tips

  • Camera at roughly floor level, whole body in frame, side view, one person. The overlay tells you when the head or feet are cut off, when two people are in the frame, when you face the camera instead of side-on, and when it is too dark.
  • The first rep counts: there is no calibration, the range is measured against your own body.
  • Limits: a frontal view is not graded (you get a "turn side-on" hint instead). A sag, a pike, kneeling and a body dropped onto the floor with the arms straight are caught; a rep that rests on the floor and pushes the chest up first with the arms properly bent looks like a clean rep to 2D landmarks and is counted as one.
  • No camera? The demo clip runs the exact same pipeline on a short recording: 4 attempts, of which 2–3 are clean by a coach's eye; the last one (chest up before the hips) is a mild "worm" that the geometry lets through.
